Help measure these techniques

This helps build a public picture of how common these techniques are, shop by shop. Reports cover what shops show you as you browse, not only what you saw when adding to your cart. It does name the shop. A report says “someone saw a countdown on shein.com today.”

On product and listing pages it also reports whether you clicked Add to Cart, as one yes-or-no per page view, next to the techniques that were on screen before you decided. That is what lets the research say how often people add an item after seeing each one. It is sent as separate counts, never as a list of what one page showed together, and never with anything about the item.

It sends the shop's main domain and nothing finer — never the page, the product, the search, or the full web address. It never sends page text, prices, your account, or anything that identifies you, and the day is the most precise time it records. Only pages Vero judged to be shops can ever appear, so nowhere you visit that is not selling something is ever reported. Reports go out in batches on a six-hour timer, never at the moment something is found, and switching this off deletes anything not yet sent.
